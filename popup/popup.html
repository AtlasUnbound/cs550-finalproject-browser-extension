What is browser fingerprinting?

Websites can recognize your browser without cookies by collecting small details about it, like your screen size, fonts and graphics card. Together these details form a fingerprint that can follow you from site to site, even in a private window.

PrintShield shows which details each page checks, and makes yours look like everyone else's so you blend into the crowd.

    📊 For testers: reading amiunique.org and Cover Your Tracks results
    • Some values will always look "unique", and that is expected. The hidden drawing, 3D image and sound tests change on every visit when protected, so they can never be matched again. Cover Your Tracks labels them "randomized".
    • amiunique.org compares you with years of past visitors. A brand-new browser version looks rare there until more people update. Cover Your Tracks only uses recent visitors.
    • The two sites can disagree. A UTC time zone, for example, is common on amiunique.org but rare on Cover Your Tracks. PrintShield aims for values that are common in real browsers today.
    • Type the test site's address yourself instead of opening it from a Google result, and don't reload it. Reloads and revisits add cache details that no extension can remove.
    • Reload the test page after changing settings, because some protections only apply to pages loaded afterwards.
